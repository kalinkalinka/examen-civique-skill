# Mises en situation — scenario guidance

The French civic exam includes **12 *mises en situation*** (situational questions) mixed in with the 28 published knowledge questions, for a total of 40 questions on exam day.

**These 12 scenarios are not published.** They cannot be memorized. They test whether the candidate can *apply* the rules from the published material to plausible everyday situations.

This file provides guidance for the skill to generate plausible scenarios (for mock exam mode) and for users to reason through them on exam day.

---

## How scenarios are structured

Each scenario presents a real-world situation in 1–3 sentences, then asks what the candidate should do, or what is legally correct. Four multiple-choice options follow, only one of which is correct.

**Typical scenario format:**

> Votre voisin vous insulte publiquement à cause de votre apparence. Que devez-vous faire ?
>
> A. Insulter votre voisin en retour.
> B. Porter plainte auprès de la police ou de la gendarmerie — c'est une injure publique punie par la loi.
> C. Ne rien faire pour ne pas avoir de problèmes.
> D. Résoudre la situation par la violence.
>
> ✓ **B**

The correct answer is almost always:
- The legally correct action (respect la loi, porter plainte, appeler les secours)
- The civic-minded action (respecter la laïcité, l'égalité, la liberté d'autrui)
- The institutionally correct path (aller à la mairie, contacter France Travail, demander l'aide juridictionnelle)

The distractors are typically:
- Inaction ("ne rien faire")
- Self-resolution outside the legal system ("régler le problème soi-même")
- Disproportionate response ("répondre par la violence")
- Religious or cultural exception ("refuser à cause de ma religion")

## Pattern library

The scenarios cluster around 8 testable themes. The skill can generate plausible scenarios in each cluster for mock exams.

### 1. Discrimination / égalité

Someone is treated differently because of their origin, sex, religion, sexual orientation, handicap, or apparence.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- A landlord refuses to rent to someone because of their nationality → illégal, porter plainte
- An employer refuses to hire women → discrimination illégale
- A school refuses to enroll a disabled child → droit à la scolarisation, aménagements
- Someone is insulted publicly for their appearance → injure publique, porter plainte

*Always-correct frames:* signaler, porter plainte, contacter le Défenseur des droits, respecter le principe d'égalité.

### 2. Laïcité / religion

Questions about religious expression in public services, public schools, workplace.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- A student wants to wear an ostentatious religious sign at public school → interdit (loi 2004)
- A parent wants to refuse mixed PE for their child for religious reasons → refus illegal, instruction obligatoire
- A civil servant wants to display religious signs while at work → interdit, neutralité de l'État
- An employee wants religious time off → can request (congés), but employer can refuse for legitimate business reasons (no discrimination)
- Someone wants to change religion → droit, liberté de conscience
- A user wears religious signs in a public service → OK (only agents must be neutral, not users)

*Always-correct frames:* respecter la laïcité (the institutional/agent neutrality), respecter la liberté individuelle (personal choice), distinguer **agent public** (must be neutral) from **usager** (free).

### 3. Liberté d'expression / réseaux sociaux

What can and cannot be said publicly.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- Someone posts an insult or hate speech on social media → puni par la loi
- A journalist publishes information critical of the government → liberté de la presse, OK dans le respect de la loi
- Someone says "freedom of expression means I can say anything" → faux, limité par la loi
- A protest is organized in a public space → droit de manifester (dans le respect de la loi)

*Always-correct frames:* liberté d'expression existe **dans le respect des limites légales** (injures, diffamation, appels à la haine, apologie du terrorisme).

### 4. Police / autorité

What to do when interacting with police or witnessing crime.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- Police give an order you disagree with → respecter d'abord, contester par voie légale après
- You witness a crime or violence → prévenir police/secours
- You're a victim of violence → porter plainte
- Someone is in immediate danger → appeler les secours (15/17/18/112), porter assistance dans la mesure du possible
- You hesitate to help because of fear → non-assistance à personne en danger = délit

*Always-correct frames:* respecter les ordres légaux, prévenir les autorités, ne jamais ignorer les violences.

### 5. École / instruction

Obligations and rights around children's education.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- Parents refuse to send their child to school for religious or personal reasons → illégal, instruction obligatoire 3–16
- A child is sick and misses school → motif légitime (maladie), prévenir l'école
- Parents want to homeschool → possible but requires authorization, instruction must still be provided
- A child with a disability wants to attend regular school → droit, aménagements
- Parents want to dictate curriculum based on religion → non, neutralité de l'école publique

*Always-correct frames:* instruction obligatoire 3–16, droit pour tous les enfants, respect de la laïcité.

### 6. Travail

Labor rights and duties.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- An employer offers undeclared work (« travail au noir ») → illégal, refuser
- An employer asks for overtime → heures supplémentaires, payées ou récupérées
- An employee wants to join a union → droit syndical
- Workers want to strike → droit de grève dans le respect de la loi
- An employee is fired and wants to challenge it → conseil de prud'hommes
- Loss of job → France Travail + allocations chômage (si conditions remplies)

*Always-correct frames:* droits du travail dans le cadre légal, conseil de prud'hommes pour conflits, France Travail pour chômage.

### 7. Santé et services sociaux

Healthcare and social services access.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- Someone without French nationality needs medical care → accès aux soins pour tous (Assurance maladie si résidence régulière)
- A doctor shares confidential medical info → violation du secret médical, puni par la loi
- A pregnant person wants information about IVG → droit garanti
- A person with low income needs legal help → aide juridictionnelle
- A child is in danger → appeler 119 (enfance en danger) ou 17

*Always-correct frames:* accès universel aux soins, secret médical, aides sociales pour les ressources modestes.

### 8. Vie quotidienne, environnement, voisinage

Everyday civic behavior.

*Common scenario shapes:*
- Someone wants to burn waste in their garden → interdit, déchetterie
- A neighbor makes excessive noise → nuisance, signaler à la police municipale ou médiation
- Someone wants to throw a cigarette butt in the street → interdit, amende
- A bulky item needs disposal → déchetterie ou service de collecte spécialisé
- A child is left alone in a car → mise en danger, signaler

*Always-correct frames:* respect du voisinage, tri des déchets, signaler les situations dangereuses.

## Reasoning approach for scenarios

For any scenario, the candidate should ask:

1. **Is there a legal rule that directly applies?** (e.g., "injure publique = interdite") → follow the rule.
2. **Is there an institutional path?** (mairie, police, prud'hommes, France Travail, Défenseur des droits, aide juridictionnelle) → use the right one.
3. **Does this involve someone in danger?** → appeler les secours, porter assistance.
4. **Is there a civic principle?** (égalité, laïcité, liberté dans le respect d'autrui) → apply it.
5. **Eliminate clearly wrong answers first.** "Ne rien faire," "se faire justice soi-même," "insulter en retour," "répondre par la violence" are almost always wrong.

## Mock exam scenario generation

When running mock exam mode and 12 scenarios are needed, the skill should:

1. Select 12 scenarios distributed across the 8 pattern clusters above (aim for ~1–2 per cluster, mixing topics)
2. Generate each scenario in French, 1–3 sentences setting up the situation
3. Provide 4 multiple-choice options in French, shuffling the position of the correct answer
4. After the user submits all 40 mock answers, explain reasoning even for correct scenario answers — the scenarios test reasoning, so the explanation IS the learning

Keep scenarios grounded in everyday situations a CSP applicant would plausibly encounter: workplace, school, neighborhood, family, healthcare, government services. Avoid edge cases or specialist legal nuance.

---

## What to tell users about scenarios on exam day

- Scenarios are NOT memorizable — they test application of the rules from the knowledge questions
- The correct answer is usually the **legally correct** + **civically appropriate** action
- Wrong answers usually include: doing nothing, taking the law into your own hands, religious/cultural exception, disproportionate response
- If unsure, ask: *what would the law expect a citizen to do here?*
- Trap words: "toujours," "tous," "jamais" — absolute statements are usually wrong
- Trap framing: "my religion / my culture / my custom requires X" — personal practice doesn't override French law
